# Research Gaps


## Overview

This document records research gaps identified
from current literature exploration.

Current sources:

- P001_HAUV_Review_2022


The purpose is to identify missing knowledge,
methods, and frameworks in HAUV development.



---

# Gap 001: Lack of Unified Cross-domain Design Framework


## Observation

Current HAUV designs usually optimize
different operation modes separately.

Examples:

Fixed-wing HAUV:

- high aerial efficiency
- limited transition capability


Multi-rotor HAUV:

- stable transition
- limited endurance


## Missing

A unified design methodology that considers:

- aerial performance
- underwater performance
- transition capability


## Related Candidate Problem

CP001:

Multimodal HAUV Configuration Optimization

# Gap 002: Insufficient Understanding of Transition Dynamics


## Observation

During air-water transition,
vehicle dynamics change significantly.

Factors include:

- density difference
- buoyancy variation
- added mass effect
- hydrodynamic damping


## Missing

A comprehensive understanding and modeling method
for time-varying cross-domain dynamics.


## Related Candidate Problem

CP002:

Time-varying Hydrodynamics During Transition

# Gap 003: Limited Robustness of Cross-domain Control


## Observation

Existing control methods have achieved
simulation and experimental demonstrations.

However, real ocean environments introduce:

- wind disturbance
- wave disturbance
- nonlinear dynamics
- parameter uncertainty


## Missing

A robust control framework capable of handling
uncertain cross-domain environments.


## Related Candidate Problem

CP003:

Robust Cross-domain Motion Control

# Gap 004: Lack of Integrated Multidisciplinary Design Approach


## Observation

HAUV development requires knowledge from:

- aerodynamics
- hydrodynamics
- control theory
- structural design


## Missing

An integrated workflow that combines
knowledge from multiple engineering domains.


## Potential ANRI Relevance

AI-native research infrastructure may help organize
and connect multidisciplinary knowledge
for cross-domain vehicle design.
