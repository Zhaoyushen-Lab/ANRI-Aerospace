# Research Log: First Strategy Update

Date:
2026-08-25

Project:
ANRI-CDFV

## Background

During the initial development of ANRI-CDFV, the project was positioned as an exploration of AI-native research infrastructure applied to cross-domain flight vehicle design.

Recent developments in AI for Science indicate that scientific research is moving from AI-assisted tools toward more integrated AI-native research workflows.

This motivates the exploration of how AI-native infrastructure can support complex engineering research.

---

## Strategic Positioning

ANRI-CDFV is positioned at the intersection of:

- AI-Native Research Infrastructure
- Cross-Domain Flight Vehicle Design
- Aerospace Engineering
- Ocean Engineering
- Control and Simulation

The project does not aim to build a general AI research platform.

Instead, it focuses on exploring a vertical application of AI-native scientific workflows in aerospace engineering.

---

## Why Cross-Domain Flight Vehicle?

Cross-domain flight vehicles represent a highly complex engineering challenge.

Unlike conventional aerial or underwater vehicles, CDFVs must operate across different physical environments.

Key challenges include:

- Aerodynamic and hydrodynamic coupling
- Air-water transition dynamics
- Nonlinear and time-varying control problems
- Multi-disciplinary design optimization

Because of these characteristics, CDFV provides a challenging validation scenario for AI-native research infrastructure.

---

## First Research Direction

The first knowledge node is based on:

"Review of hybrid aerial underwater vehicle: Cross-domain mobility and transitions control"

Initial observations:

- HAUVs enable air-water cross-domain operation.
- Existing designs include bio-inspired, fixed-wing, multi-rotor, and multimodal approaches.
- Cross-domain transition control remains one of the key challenges.

This paper establishes the initial engineering knowledge foundation for ANRI-CDFV.

---

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term goal is to explore a workflow where AI can assist in:

1. Scientific knowledge extraction
2. Engineering knowledge organization
3. Multi-physics design analysis
4. Concept generation and optimization

The ultimate objective is to investigate how aerospace engineering research can evolve toward an AI-native workflow.
